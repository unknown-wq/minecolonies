# flat-noise

A datapack that flattens the overworld surface, cuts rivers back into it, and leaves everything
under it alone.

Five files, each replacing one vanilla density function. Nothing else about worldgen changes:
caves, aquifers, the lava sea, ore, structures and biomes are generated by the same functions as
in an ordinary world.

| File | Vanilla | Here |
|---|---|---|
| `overworld/depth.json` | y gradient **+ the offset spline** over continentalness, erosion and ridges | the same y gradient **+ a constant − a river carve** |
| `overworld/base_3d_noise.json` | `old_blended_noise` | `0` |
| `overworld/jaggedness.json` | noise, gated on peaks | `0` |
| `overworld/preliminary_surface_level.json` | `find_top_surface` over its own inlined copy of gradient + offset | `66 − the same river carve` |

The offset spline is what makes hills, valleys and ocean basins; the 3D noise is what makes
overhangs and metre-scale roughness. Remove both and the density still changes sign at exactly
one height, and it is now the same height everywhere. Land ends up at y=66.

## Rivers

Removing the spline removes rivers with it, and a colony wants water: a fisher's hut needs a
7x7x2 pool and a supply ship 32x20. So one term of the spline is put back by hand.

Vanilla's peaks-and-valleys parameter is `overworld/ridges_folded`, which is
`-3 * (| |ridges| - 2/3 | - 1/3)`. It reaches -1 exactly where the ridge noise crosses zero, and
that thin ribbon is where vanilla puts its river biomes. Carving the terrain down along the same
ribbon puts the channel where the biome already is, so `minecraft:river` lands on an actual river.

The carve is `clamp((-0.72 - ridges_folded) * 200, 0, 14)` blocks: a flat bottom 14 below the
plain across most of the ribbon, with short walls at the edges. Land 66, bed 52, water to 62.

Getting the channel wet is the part that is not obvious. Water does not come from the density at
all -- it comes from `Aquifer`, which only floods a cell when the fluid at its estimated surface
is not air (`Aquifer:481`), and that estimate is `preliminary_surface_level + 8`
(`Aquifer:503`). Below sea level 63 the global picker gives water, above it gives air, so a
channel fills only where the surface estimate is **at least 9 below sea level** -- 54 or lower.
That is why the carve is 14 blocks and not 6, and why the fourth file subtracts the same carve
from the surface estimate instead of being a constant: a constant low enough to wet the rivers
declares the whole world to be under water and floods the top of the cave system with it.

## The fourth file, in general

Vanilla's surface estimate does not read `overworld/depth` -- it recomputes the density from its
own copy of the gradient and the offset spline -- so overriding depth alone leaves it predicting
terrain that no longer exists. Besides the aquifer it is the lower bound for the surface rule's
downward scan (`SurfaceRules:433`, `minSurfaceLevel = this + surfaceDepth - 8`). Too high and the
scan never reaches the ground, leaving bare stone; too low only costs iterations.

## Where the surface ends up

The density crosses zero at y=65 and the topmost solid block is y=66, one above. The interpolator
samples the density every 8 blocks and lerps between the samples, and `quarter_negative` inside
`sloped_cheese` scales negative values by 0.25 -- so the sample above the surface is four times
smaller in magnitude than the one below, and the interpolated crossing lands high. Vanilla behaves
the same way; the constant in `depth.json` was chosen with that in mind. To move the surface,
change the constant: 0.0078125 is one block. Move the 66.0 in the surface estimate with it.

## Measured

361 fully generated chunks per seed, 26.3-snapshot-9, read out of the region files:

* **flat**: top terrain block at y=66 in 79.9% of columns on seed 4242 and 86.2% on seed 90210,
  the remainder rivers, cave mouths and the odd boulder;
* **caves**: 5.5% of the volume below y=48 is air, i.e. normal density;
* **ore**: coal 30k, copper 28k, iron 19k + 10k deepslate, lapis 3.9k, gold 1.7k, redstone 971,
  diamond 129 + 9.4k deepslate;
* **lava** peaks at y=-56, the vanilla lava sea, and obsidian is already present where it met
  water -- so the Nether is reachable, which is what a superflat world cannot offer;
* **water**: 14.5% of the area on seed 4242, 3.3% on seed 90210; largest unbroken water surface
  42x42 and 21x21 respectively, either of which holds a fisher's hut;
* **biomes**: 5 distinct on seed 4242 (all snowy), 7 on seed 90210 (birch forest, forest, grove,
  frozen peaks, river, lush caves). Variety survives the flattening, because biome choice never
  depended on terrain height;
* **structures**: mineshafts and a ruined portal in the sample.

Seed matters more than usual, since the biome is the only thing left varying: on a snowy seed the
rivers freeze over, and a fisherman cannot fish through ice. Pin `MC_LEVEL_SEED` and look before
committing to a world.

## Use

`MC_FLAT_NOISE=1 tools/run-server.sh <jar>` installs it into the test world and switches the level
type to `minecraft:normal`. The pack must be in place before the chunks are generated; the script
regenerates the world when the mode changes.

Written against 26.3-snapshot-9, pack format 117. The density function schema is version-specific:
26.3 uses `minecraft:gradient` with `from_coordinate`/`to_coordinate` and `left`/`right`, where
earlier versions used `minecraft:y_clamped_gradient` with `from_y`/`to_y` and
`argument1`/`argument2`.
